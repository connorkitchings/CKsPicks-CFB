### 1. Play-by-play running score is not monotonic (opened 2026-10-02)

- **What:** each Silver `byplay` row carries a running score for both teams (`offense_score`, `defense_score`). For many team-games the score goes down from one row to the next, which cannot happen in football. Example (Vanderbilt, 2026): 28 to 27 after a blocked-punt touchdown row (Delaware, game 401856684), 35 to 28 after a fumble-return touchdown row (NC State, 401856695), and 15 to 12 on a penalty row with no scoring play nearby (Auburn, 401856698). Final scores still reconcile exactly.
- **How often:** about a third of team-games are flagged every week (`ppso_invalid_offenses` in the `publish_team_stats.py` report): week 1 7 of 16, week 2 36 of 102, week 3 72 of 200, week 4 98 of 314, week 5 133 of 430. The steady 31-36% rate suggests a systematic pattern in the feed, not random bad games.
- **Effect:** `true_drive_points` (`ratings/observations.py`) quarantines an offense in any game whose score stream decreases, so that game is dropped from `pts_per_scoring_opp`. Teams keep a value from their remaining games (thinner sample); a team with every game flagged shows "—" with no rank (Vanderbilt through week 4; about 4 offenses and 7 defenses at week 5). No other team-stats metric uses the score.
- **Cause (investigated 2026-10-03, read-only; working notes `artifacts/backups/2026-10-03/investigation/a1-findings.md`):** the dips are in CFBD's raw feed, not introduced by our Silver build. The raw Bronze captures hold 272 drop events in 158 team-games (Silver 223 in 133) and have **zero null running scores** out of 58,379 plays, so the `fillna(0)` on scores is not the cause. Re-sorting by wallclock or period/clock does not remove them (still 133 team-games by wallclock), so it is not a sort artifact. Silver classes (223 events): 42 drop to exactly 0, 31 one-or-two-point drops after a scoring row (the PAT credited before the kick, e.g. Vanderbilt 28 to 27), 15 other drops after a scoring row, 20 next to a Penalty row, 115 on rows where the previous row did not score (TDs and field goals credited, then reversed). 219 of the 223 are later restored to at least the pre-drop value.
- **Correction:** "final scores still reconcile exactly" is not true for every game. For 6 of 215 games the maximum running score differs from the final (Army 30 vs 24 against South Florida, Pittsburgh/UCF, Michigan State/Toledo, Boise State/Memphis, Middle Tennessee/Nevada, New Mexico State/New Mexico), and 4 dips never restore. Any correction rule that trusts the restored value must be capped by the game final.
- **V5 impact (investigated 2026-10-03; `artifacts/backups/2026-10-03/investigation/b-findings.md`):** the count in the Week 5 audit update below ("129 measurements") counted measurement rows. The real number is **27 of 430 team-games (6.3%)**, in 24 games and 24 teams, marked `unresolved_scoring_attribution` and `rating_usable = False`; the ledger's rollback absorbs most of the 133 flagged streams. Their stored `offensive_possession_points` total 234 although those teams scored 899 points (10 of the 27 store exactly 0, e.g. USC 39 vs Fresno State, Ohio State 59 vs Kent State). Quarantined games average about 3.1 points per possession (upper bound) against 2.15 league-wide, so the exclusion biases affected offenses down. Simulating the lift on the published raw `ppp`: 23-24 teams move by more than 0.05 and 14-22 teams move by more than 5 ranks per role even in the conservative scenario (Wisconsin offense 1.68 to 3.00, rank 92 to 24; Purdue 77 to 38; Wake Forest 40 to 15; Kent State 138 to 121). The D4 flip criterion is met at the raw level, so D4 is **proposed** as "now" pending the rating-level delta report (not decided); any rebuild needs its own contract. The rating-level effect (opponent-adjusted and prior-shrunk) is smaller and is not yet measured.
- **Per-game points identity (2026-10-03, local rebuild of the scoring ledger from the w4 Silver set, reproduces the served quarantine 27 of 27):** 403 of 430 team-games reconcile exactly to the final score; of the 27 that do not, 24 are quarantined and 3 are observed (the 3 other quarantined games, Army W2, Memphis W2 and Middle Tennessee W3, reconcile and are quarantined only by the final-score cap). Kent State W3's 3 points are a field goal on a possession with no eligible plays and sit in `excluded_regulation_offense` by design (474 points league-wide, 4.2%, are in that bucket and in neither `ppp` numerator). Three team-games lose points silently while staying `observed`, so they feed `ppp` understated: Vanderbilt W3 vs NC State (7), Northern Illinois W3 (7), New Mexico State W4 (12), all from the score-regression rollback.
- **Rating-level delta, Tier 1 (2026-10-03, in memory, priors and the 2025 terminal table fixed; `artifacts/backups/2026-10-03/investigation/delta-findings.md`):** the pinned builder and rating engine reproduce the served observations (6,880 of 6,880 rows) and the served Week 5 ratings (112 teams, zero difference) exactly, so the delta is valid. A candidate rule R1 (monotone envelope capped at the game final; frozen in `d1-candidate-rule.md` before the run) lifts the points identity from 403 to 411 of 430 team-games with no regressions and cuts quarantines from 27 to 18 (the remaining 18 are single-row jumps above 8 points, which R1 does not address). Its rating effect splits into channel A, points actually recovered (11 team-games, +113 offensive points): 18 teams move by more than 0.05 overall rating (sd 0.56), 15 teams by more than 5 ranks (max 36), and an approximate bridge (R2 0.997 on margin) moves 16 of 56 Week 5 margins by more than a point with 4 spread-side flips; and channel B, attribution-only changes in 134 team-games that already reconciled (mean absolute change 2.2 offensive points), which moves more teams (67) but cannot be validated. So R1 as written is not a safe rebuild input. **Revised the same day, twice (read-only; `d1-candidate-rule.md`, `quarter-scores-findings.md`).** (1) CFBD `drives` (4,961 drives) is not a clean oracle: drive scores are monotone in 383 of 430 team-games, drive points exceed the final in 36, and it is clean in only 9 of the 30 affected team-games; on CFBD-clean team-games it agrees with the baseline ledger more often than with R1 where they differ (36 vs 28 of 66 drives), so channel B is unsupported; the three "silently short" team-games (Vanderbilt W3, Northern Illinois W3, New Mexico State W4) are non-drive scores (defensive or special-teams touchdowns) that feed the forecast offsets and the matchup page, not `ppp`. D4 is proposed \"now\" at that scope pending its contract. Total-score movement could not be estimated reliably (R2 0.82). The effect of the 2025 quarantines on the 2026 priors (Tier 2) was not measured.
- **Suggested next steps:** (1) compare the raw CFBD plays with Silver for the three Vanderbilt games and classify the dips; (2) have the publish step list flagged games per week and warn when the rate jumps; (3) check V5 `ppp` for the same games; (4) only then consider treating corrected dips as valid, which changes the metric definition and needs a contract.

### 3. Zero-PPA plays that may be missing values

155 eligible non-punt plays in 2026 Silver have `ppa == 0` exactly, probably CFBD nulls turned into zeros by Silver's `fillna(0)` (`features/byplay/enrichment.py`). They sit inside every per-play PPA mean, including `ppa_per_play`. Decide at the Silver rebuild whether to treat them as nulls (batch step 4).

**Scope note:** raw `ppa` is null on 7,213 of 35,407 weeks 0-4 Silver rows (20.4%), but those are almost all kickoffs (2,279), penalties (2,130), punts (1,237), field goals (516) and similar rows where CFBD gives no EPA by design; they are excluded from every EPA metric. Only the 155 eligible scrimmage plays below matter.

**Investigated 2026-10-03 (`artifacts/backups/2026-10-03/investigation/d-findings.md`):** all 155 (100%) are CFBD nulls filled with 0; none is a genuine zero. They are mostly turnover-type plays (41 fumble recoveries, 13 interceptions, 41 rushes, 26 receptions). For displayed team stats, treating them as missing changes nothing visible (largest value change 0.014, no rank moves more than 4). The zero-fill biases EPA-derived measures: 127 of 430 offense team-games (29.5%) carry plays counted as exactly 0 EPA whose typical value is strongly negative (about -232 EPA over the 155 plays by same-play-type reference means). The serving V5 rating fits PPP, not EPA; affected EPA must nevertheless be corrected in the Window 2 measurement lineage. Making nulls null in Silver alone would withhold EPA for those 127 team-games.

**Cause of the nulls (addendum 2026-10-03, `d-findings.md`):** 108 of the 155 have no valid pricing state or are not plays (10 offsetting-penalty no-plays, 11 turnovers on kick/punt returns, 12 end-zone touchbacks, 75 with a missing start state), 47 have a valid state. The nulls are systematic by situation, not by play type alone (CFBD prices 68% of opponent fumble recoveries and 86% of interceptions). CFBD's documentation says turnovers are priced from their resulting game state and that a null "should not be converted to zero without an explicit analytical reason". No null was ever filled by a later capture (0 of 155 across 35 captures; 0 of 67 in a fresh week-3 pull), so a re-fetch will not help. About 70 turnover-type plays carry almost all of the bias (about -240 EPA). The earlier imputation recommendation is superseded by the recorded user decision: do not impute; exclude ineligible rows and withhold affected EPA with an explicit reason. Also found: CFBD revised turnover `ppa` in mid-September (682 plays in weeks 1-2, mean change 2.1 EPA); Silver holds the corrected values, but the weekly cycle should refresh earlier weeks before freezing (D9).

### 9. Neutral-site games get the full home edge (opened 2026-10-03; model issue, not a data fix)

The V5 forecast bridge never sees venue: `home_host=1.0` and `venue_unknown=True` are hard-coded (`forecast/live.py:172-173`, `forecast/historical_features.py:121`, `ratings/possession_rating_materializer.py:1201,1325`), the columns never vary so they are dropped (`live.py` non-varying filter), and the home edge lives only in the margin intercept (7.437 in the served bundle, agent-reported). Neutral games are therefore forecast as home games. Evidence (`g5-g7-findings.md`): in 2026 weeks 0-4 the 6 neutral games average **+5.4 points (SE 2.0) more toward the home team than the market** and +11.3 versus the actual margin, against -1.25 and -1.7 for the 209 non-neutral games (verified directly from Neon `predictions`, `game_venues`, `game_results`; n=6, individual gaps range from -0.1 to +11.8). The 2025 holdout (a v4 replay, not V5) is directionally the same: +4.2 (SE 1.8) over the market on 22 neutral games vs -1.0 on 739 others (agent-reported; 5 of the 22 are Sam Houston "home" games at BBVA). Agent-reported sizing: roughly +3 to +3.5 points per neutral game toward the home team (plausible range 2 to 5.5); an outside Elo regression gives a neutral effect of about -2.85 points (SE 1.5, n=118). The sample is small, so this is suggestive, not proven. Oklahoma-Texas (Week 6) is the first neutral game still to be forecast; Navy-Notre Dame and Georgia-Florida (Week 9), EMU-CMU (Week 10) and Army-Navy (Week 15) follow. Proposal D8: a separate model contract (add a neutral-site term or shrink the intercept for neutral games); nothing is changed here.
